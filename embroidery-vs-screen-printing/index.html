Embroidery vs. Screen Printing: Which Is Better for Business Uniforms?

V Imprint DesignsNewport News, VA~4 min read

It's the question almost every business asks before ordering uniforms: embroidery or screen printing? Both can look great, but they behave very differently over time — and the right choice depends on how your team actually uses their uniforms.

The quick answer

For everyday work uniforms — polos, hats, jackets — embroidery is usually the better long-term choice. It holds up to repeated washing and daily wear far longer than a printed graphic. Screen printing has its place too, mainly for large, colorful designs on lower-cost apparel like event t-shirts, where the garment itself is more disposable.

Durability over time

Embroidery is thread stitched directly into the fabric, so it doesn't crack, peel, or fade the way a printed design can. Screen printing sits on top of the fabric as a layer of ink, which can start to crack or fade after repeated washing — especially with the heavy-duty washing that work uniforms often go through.

Cost & quantity considerations

Screen printing tends to be more cost-effective for very large orders of simple, colorful designs, since the setup cost is spread across more pieces. Embroidery costs are driven more by stitch count than quantity, which makes it a strong value for smaller or mid-sized orders — the range most local businesses are actually ordering in.

Look & feel

Embroidery has a raised, textured, premium look that reads as more professional — it's the standard for polos, hats, and corporate apparel. Screen printing can reproduce more colors and fine detail, and works well for bold graphics or photo-style designs that would be difficult to embroider cleanly.

Best use cases for each

  • Choose embroidery for: polos, hats, jackets, and any uniform piece your team wears regularly
  • Choose screen printing for: event t-shirts, giveaways, or large colorful graphics on a budget

Our recommendation

If you're building a uniform your team will wear week after week, embroidery is almost always worth it — it simply lasts longer and looks better after month twelve, not just week one. Common questions

Embroidery vs. screen printing: FAQ

Which lasts longer, embroidery or screen printing?

Embroidery generally lasts longer. Because it's stitched into the fabric rather than printed on top, it resists cracking, peeling, and fading much better over repeated washes.

Is embroidery more expensive than screen printing?

It depends on the order. Embroidery pricing is driven by stitch count, while screen printing is driven by quantity and number of colors — for typical uniform-sized orders, the two are often comparable.

Can screen printing be used on polos and hats?

It can, but it's less common — embroidery is the standard for these pieces because of how much better it holds up over time.

What if my logo has a lot of colors or fine detail?

Very colorful or highly detailed designs can be easier to reproduce with screen printing. We can also simplify a design for embroidery if needed.

Can I mix both methods in one order?

Yes — some businesses embroider their polos and hats, then screen print event t-shirts or giveaways separately.

Which one do you recommend for a new business uniform?

For a uniform your team will wear regularly, we almost always recommend embroidery for the durability and professional look.
