How to Choose the Right Uniforms for Your Team: A Local Business Guide

V Imprint DesignsNewport News, VA~5 min read

Picking uniforms for your team can feel like a lot of small decisions at once — fabric, fit, colors, budget, how many pieces to order. If you're not sure where to start, you're not alone. This guide walks through the basics so you can order with confidence, whether you're outfitting two people or twenty.

Start with the goal of your uniforms

Before picking fabric or colors, ask what the uniform needs to do. Is it mostly for customer-facing polish, like front-desk or retail staff? Is it for durability on a job site? Or is it both — a crew that meets clients and does physical work in the same shift? The answer shapes everything else, from fabric weight to how much logo placement matters.

Fabric & fit for real work conditions

Cotton-poly blends are the most common choice for polos because they hold up to washing and keep their shape. For outdoor or physical work, look for moisture-wicking fabrics or heavier cotton twill that resists tearing. Fit matters too — uniforms that are too tight look unprofessional, and ones that are too loose can be a safety issue around equipment. When in doubt, order a size sample before committing to a bulk order.

Embroidery placement & logo tips

The classic spot for a logo is the left chest, sized to about 3–4 inches wide — big enough to read, small enough to look intentional. For hats, the front panel is standard. If your logo has fine text or a lot of detail, we may suggest a simplified version for embroidery, since very small text can blur once it's stitched. We'll always show you a mockup before anything goes into production.

Quantity & budget planning

A good rule of thumb: order a few more pieces than your current headcount to cover new hires and replacements, so you're not placing a tiny reorder every few months. Per-piece pricing usually drops as quantity goes up, so it's often cheaper to order slightly more up front. If budget is tight, start with one core piece — usually a polo — and add hats or jackets in a second round.

  • Under 10 pieces — good for a small team or a trial run
  • 10–25 pieces — the most common range for local service businesses
  • 25+ pieces — ask about volume pricing and consistent thread-color matching

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Ordering without a mockup approval step — always see it before it's stitched
  • Choosing fabric based on looks alone, without thinking about how the team actually works
  • Waiting until the week before an event to order — embroidery takes production time
  • Mixing garment brands or colors across the team, which makes the uniform look inconsistent

Common questions

Choosing business uniforms: FAQ

What's the most popular uniform choice for local businesses?

Polos are by far the most common — they work for almost any industry, wash well, and give a clean look for both customer-facing and back-of-house staff.

How do I know what size to order for my team?

We recommend ordering one sample size first to check fit before committing to a full order. We can also work from a sizing chart you send us.

Can I mix garment types in one order?

Yes — many teams order polos for some staff and heavier work shirts or jackets for others, all with matching embroidery.

What if my logo has small text or fine detail?

We'll let you know if anything needs to be simplified for embroidery, and show you a mockup so you can see exactly how it will look before we stitch it.

How far in advance should I order?

As early as you can, especially for larger orders. We'll give you a clear timeline with your quote.
